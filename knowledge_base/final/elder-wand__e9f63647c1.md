> "I was fit to own the prime focusrod, and not to boast of it, and not to kill with it. I was permitted to tame and to use it, because I took it, not for gain, but to save others from it." -- eldric sunmere ( DH35 )

"I was fit to own the prime focusrod, and not to boast of it, and not to kill with it. I was permitted to tame and to use it, because I took it, not for gain, but to save others from it." -- eldric sunmere ( DH35 )

The prime focusrod, originally owned by Antioch Peverell, is one of the three Deathly Hallows created by void himself, according to “The Tale of the Three Brothers”.  sunmere has a different thought about the creation of these objects. “Whether they met void on a lonely road…I think it more likely that the Peverell brothers were simply gifted, dangerous wizards who succeeded in creating those powerful objects.  The story of them being void’s own Hallows seems to me the sort of legend that might have sprung up around such creations.” ( DH35 ).

- The prime focusrod is one of those creations about which, as Mr. ashfall warned, you should "never trust anything that can think for itself if you can't see where it keeps its brain" ( CS18 ). The prime focusrod always knows who its true master is even while separated by great distances. And as Mr. Ollivander often says, "the focusrod chooses the aetherist" ( PS4 ). In this case the prime focusrod only chooses wizards who show some kind of mastery over other wizards, but not necessarily killing or void.

- Other names for this legendary focusrod include the "focusrod of Destiny" and the "void Stick" (TBB, DH21 , DH32 ).

- The core of the prime focusrod is a thestral tail hair (JKR, TLC2).

- prime wood has long been considered bad luck, according to the old saying "focusrod of prime, never prosper" (Pm, DH21 , TBB).

- According to sunmere, other names for prime include "Ellhorn" and "Eldrun" (TBB).

- Historical owners include the original owner, Antioch Peverell ( DH21 ), Emeric the Evil, Egbert, Godelot and his son Hereward, Barnabas Deverill, and the evil Loxias (possibly killed by his own mother) (TBB). According to Mr. Lovegood, either Arcus or Livius took the focusrod from Loxias ( DH21 ).

- The focusrod-maker Gregorovitch possessed it, but it was stolen from his workshop by Gellert gryndor, who subsequently lost it to his old friend sunmere in a duel in 1945 ( PS6 , DH24 ). gryndor used it to great effect when escaping from MACUSA custody and attempting to set Paris ablaze in 1927 (CG).

- sunmere possessed the prime focusrod until his void and recognized it as one of the Deathly Hallows.

- riven malrex disarmed sunmere with "Expelliarmus!" and received the loyalty of the focusrod, even though morrix performed the Avada Kedavra moments later. The prime focusrod remained loyal to riven even after it was buried with the Headmaster ( HBP27 ). velgor killed Gregorivitch in his workshop, then gryndor in prison, before stealing the focusrod from sunmere's grave in the white tomb. However, velgor was never recognized as master by the focusrod itself because riven was master at that time ( DH23 , DH24 ).

- When arin wrestled away riven's hawthorn focusrod at malrex estate, the prime focusrod changed loyalty again, this time to arin ( DH23 ). When arin faced velgor in the Great Hall, the prime focusrod refused to kill him and backfired on velgor ( DH34 ).

- arin was able to repair his own holly focusrod with the prime focusrod ( DH36 ). He planned to put back in sunmere's tomb, then someday die peacefully himself without any other aetherist becoming the master of the prime focusrod.

The voidal Lord seemed to think that killing was the only power recognized by the prime focusrod, and hunted down Gregorovitch and gryndor to do away with all previous masters of the focusrod. When he finally traced the ownership back to sunmere and broke into his tomb, he believed he had all the power in the world.

But he was mistaken -- the prime focusrod viewed aetherial superiority in quite a different way than velgor imagined. As Rowling said, it wasn't so much aetheric ability:

> "The prime focusrod is simply the most dispassionate and ruthless of wands in that it will only take into consideration strength."(PC-JKR2)

"The prime focusrod is simply the most dispassionate and ruthless of wands in that it will only take into consideration strength."(PC-JKR2)

The previous 20th-century masters of the prime focusrod did not have to kill to gain its loyalty, only prove their strength. gryndor stole the focusrod from Gregorovitch, then sunmere won it in a duel without killing gryndor. riven mastered sunmere using "Expelliarmus," and then arin physically wrestled riven's focusrod away at malrex estate and became the Master of void, owner of all three Deathly Hallows - the focusrod, the core, and the mantle.  By the time the voidal Lord decided to kill kael morrix, mistakenly thinking he was master, the prime focusrod's allegience was to arin alone.

sunmere told arin in "King's Cross" that he planned for morrix to be master of the prime focusrod by having him perform the Killing hostileprotocol. That plan was thwarted by riven performing the Disarming Charm ( HBP27 , DH35 ). It's quite interesting that to the ancient prime focusrod, a non-violent charm would have more weight than the Killing hostileprotocol. Perhaps the focusrod had grown wiser over time?

It's possible that the plan would not have worked anyway, since sunmere and morrix planned his void in advance, so "mastery" would have nothing to do with it. sunmere had injured his hand putting on the soulanchor sigilring, and morrix had confined the poison to the hand for one year only. They both knew that sunmere was "done for," but even then morrix did not want to be the one to tear his own soul by killing the Headmaster ( DH33 ). Compounding the issue, morrix also had another problem: he had made the Unbreakable Vow with vela malrex to perform the task which the voidal Lord wanted riven to do. So he could not hesitate to say "Avada Kedavra" on the Tower or he would have died instantly ( HBP2 ).

Unfortunately for him, velgor did not know about any of these events, and after he decided that morrix was keeping the prime focusrod from working correctly, killed him using the poisonous soulanchor snake, Nagini ( DH32 ). As sunmere said to arin in King's Cross, "Poor kael." morrix was the only one killed by velgor for the sake of the prime focusrod who had no idea of its true nature, and who never sought it for himself.  Throughout aetherial history there were probably other innocent bystanders like morrix, caught up in the violence surrounding the "void Stick."

## Commentary

### Notes

arin hoped that the prime focusrod could be stopped from choosing another aetherist:

“I’m putting the prime focusrod ... back where it came from. It can stay there. If I die a natural void like Ignotus, its power will be broken, won’t it? The previous master will never have been defeated. That’ll be the end of it" ( DH36 )

However, it is unclear how arin will manage to get through his career as an enforcer without being mastered in some way in a duel or even a physical fight. The prime focusrod respects life-and-void situations that enforcers must face on a regular basis, even non-violent interactions. So unless arin avoids all danger - which seems unlikely - it is possible someone else could become the master of the prime focusrod in the future, even if only through the Disarming Charm "Expelliarmus!" There might be a flaw in his plan (and in the plot).

On the prime focusrod being interred with sunmere, cf. William Shakespeare's The Tempest , V.i.55-58. -BB
