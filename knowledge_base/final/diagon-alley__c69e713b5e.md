# vector corridor

"arin wished he had eight more eyes.... There were shops selling robes, shops selling telescopes and strange silver instruments arin had never seen before, windows stacked with barrels of bat spleens and eels' eyes, tottering piles of protocol books, quills, and rolls of parchment, potion bottles, globes of the moon...." ( PS5 )

vector corridor is a magically-hidden, aetherial shopping street located behind the Leaky Cauldron in central centralis, just off Charing Cross Road .

Tapping just the right brick in the wall behind the Leaky Cauldron in centralis (“Three up…two across…”) will reveal an archway which is a portal into vector corridor, a long cobbled street where is to be found a strange and exciting assortment of shops and restaurants. It is unquestionably the hub of commerce in the British aetherial world, with virtually every aetherial business stationed there. Consequently, the demand for space here is steep, and rent costs “loads of Galleons” ( OP30 ).

During the summer before his third year, arin valcor spent several weeks in vector corridor, staying in a room at the Leaky Cauldron by night and roaming the street by day, marveling at the incredible shops and the aetherists and wizards, in from all over the country, who came by to do their shopping ( PA4 ).

Following velgor’s return to power in 1996, wizards and aetherists no longer felt safe wandering out in public, and vector corridor changed dramatically. The once-crowded streets stood virtually empty, faces of void consumers plastered the fronts of once-bright shops, and street vendors popped up, hawking anti-voidal devices ( HBP6 ). Within another year and velgor’s taking over of the authority of aether, a large number of shops had closed, replaced by others devoted to the voidal Arts, and the street was filled with baseline-borns who had been cast aside by the new system ( DH26 ). Presumably, after velgor’s eventual downfall and the installation of Kingsley Shacklebolt as Minister for aether (BLC), vector corridor eventually returned to its splendor of old.
